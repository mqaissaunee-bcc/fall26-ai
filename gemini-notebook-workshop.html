    A self-paced tutorial and 90-minute hands-on workshop

    Turn your documents into podcasts and videos Gemini Notebook, the tool formerly called NotebookLM

    You bring the sources. Gemini Notebook reads only those sources, answers questions about them with citations, and turns them into audio conversations, narrated videos, slides, quizzes, and more. This page walks you through all of it, with most of our time spent on audio and video.

    Before you start

    • Sign in with a Google account. If your school provides Google accounts, use that one. If your school uses Microsoft 365, you'll use a free personal Google account. See Choosing an account for what this means for privacy and sharing.
    • Pick 2–4 sources on one topic: a syllabus, an open textbook chapter, lecture notes, or an article. A focused set gives better results than a pile of unrelated files.
    • Use only material you have the right to upload. On a personal account, never upload student records or other private information.
    • Headphones help during the audio lab.
    Facilitator: Keep a ready-made sample notebook in case someone's files fail to upload. An open-licensed chapter works well because everyone can use it freely. Build it on a personal Google account so you can share it with a public link regardless of what accounts participants use. Ask participants to create a personal Google account before the session if their school doesn't provide one.

    What Gemini Notebook is

    0:05 – 0:10

    A research and study tool that works only from the sources you give it.

    Google renamed NotebookLM to Gemini Notebook on July 16, 2026. It is the same product with the same notebooks: only the name and logo changed, along with a few new features. Old NotebookLM links redirect automatically, so nothing you built before is lost. Many help articles and videos still use the old name, so you'll see both.

    Where to find it

    • On the web: notebook.google.com (the older notebooklm.google.com address still works).
    • On your phone: the Gemini Notebook app for Android and iOS. Some Studio features are limited on mobile.
    • Inside the Gemini app: the Notebooks section syncs with Gemini Notebook, so a notebook you build on the web can be used in a Gemini chat.

    The big idea: source-grounded AI

    A general chatbot answers from everything it learned in training. Gemini Notebook answers from your sources. Every answer includes numbered citations you can click to see the exact passage. If the answer isn't in your sources, it will usually say so instead of guessing.

    Why this matters for teaching

    Because outputs are tied to your materials, a podcast or video it makes will reflect your course content and terminology, not a generic version of the topic. That makes it far more useful for class than a general-purpose chatbot, but you should still review everything before sharing it.

    New since the rename

    • Code execution: notebooks can write and run code in a secure cloud computer to analyze data in your sources. It rolled out to the top plans first.
    • Gemini app sync: notebooks appear in the Gemini app's Notebooks section.
    • Newer model: the tool runs on Google's current Gemini models, which improves reasoning across several sources.

    Choosing an account

    0:10 – 0:14

    The tool works the same everywhere, but the account you sign in with changes your privacy protections and sharing options.

    Select the situation that matches your school.

    Your school uses Microsoft 365

    Your school email is a Microsoft account, so it can't sign in to Gemini Notebook. You'll use a free personal Google account instead. That works fine for building course materials, with a few habits to follow.

    • Check your school's AI policy first. Some institutions limit which AI tools employees may use for course work.
    • Keep it to shareable material. Your school's data agreements don't cover a personal Google account. Use your own content, openly licensed resources, and public materials. Never upload student names, work, grades, or records.
    • Deliver files, not logins. Download the audio, video, slides, or infographic and post them in your LMS, OneDrive, or Teams. Students can then use them without creating Google accounts.
    • Think before asking students to sign up. Requiring students to create personal accounts with an outside company raises privacy and equity questions. Make it optional, or check with your institution.
    • Skip the feedback buttons on anything sensitive. On personal accounts, thumbs up or down sends that conversation for human review.
    • Public links work. Personal accounts can share a notebook or overview with "Anyone with a link."

    A Microsoft option to know about

    Microsoft offers a similar tool, Copilot Notebooks, in the Microsoft 365 Copilot app and OneNote. It also grounds answers in files you add and can create audio overviews, and it works with work or school accounts under your institution's Microsoft agreement. It has fewer output types than Gemini Notebook, and which features you get depends on your school's licenses, so ask your IT department what's turned on.

    Your school uses Google Workspace for Education

    Sign in with your school Google account. Gemini Notebook is a Core Service in every Workspace for Education edition, including the free one, so it's covered by your school's existing Google agreement.

    • Stronger data protection. Uploads, questions, and responses aren't reviewed by humans or used to train AI models, even if you click the feedback buttons. Google states it supports compliance with regulations such as FERPA and COPPA.
    • Your admin controls access. Administrators can turn Gemini Notebook on or off for everyone or for specific groups. If you can't open it, contact your IT department.
    • Public sharing is off. Education accounts can't make notebooks public. Share with specific people in your school, or download files to post.
    • Drive files are copied. Adding a Drive file creates a copy stored with your notebook, and your school's Drive sharing and data-region settings don't apply to that copy.
    • Classroom connection. If your admin allows it, Gemini features also appear in Google Classroom.
    • Age limits still apply. Some features, such as Cinematic and Short videos, are limited to users 18 and older.

    Even with these protections, follow your school's rules about what student information may be used with AI.

    Using a personal Google account

    Anyone with a Google account can use Gemini Notebook free, with paid Google AI plans raising the limits.

    • Google says your notebook content isn't used to directly train its AI models unless you give feedback.
    • Clicking thumbs up or down sends the related content for review by trained people. Avoid it on anything private.
    • You get every sharing option, including public links.
    • Treat it like any consumer service: don't upload confidential or personal information.

    The interface and your sources

    0:14 – 0:25

    Every notebook has three panels. Select one below to see what it does.

    Adding sources

    1. Open a notebook, or select Create new on the home screen.
    2. Select + Add sources.
    3. Upload a file, pick one from Google Drive, paste a link, or paste text.
    4. Wait a moment. Each source gets an automatic summary and key topics.

    What you can add

    Supported source types
    TypeExamplesGood to know
    DocumentsPDF, Word (.docx), PowerPoint (.pptx), text, Markdown, CSV, EPUBGoogle Docs, Slides, and Sheets can come straight from Drive.
    WebPublic web pages, YouTube videosYouTube works through the video's captions, so the video must be public with captions.
    AudioMP3, WAV, and similarTranscribed to text when imported. Great for recorded lectures you own.
    ImagesPNG, JPEG, GIF, WEBP, TIFFUseful for diagrams, handouts, and whiteboard photos.
    Your own notesNotes you write or save from chatA note can be converted into a source so the AI uses your ideas too.

    The free plan allows 50 sources per notebook. Very large single files have size and word limits. Paid plans raise the source count.

    Choosing which sources to use

    Each source has a checkbox. Only checked sources are used by the Chat and by anything you generate in the Studio. Unchecking unrelated sources is the single easiest way to get more focused results, and it's how you make, for example, an Audio Overview of just one chapter.

    No sources yet? Let it find some

    The source panel can search the web for you.

    • Fast Research does a quick scan and suggests sources you can import.
    • Deep Research takes several minutes, browses many sites, and returns a cited report plus a list of sources to add with one click.

    Check what it finds

    Discovered sources are a starting point, not a vetted reading list. Open each one and remove anything low-quality before you build audio or video on top of it.

    Walkthrough: a Deep Research run

    1. Describe what you need. Be specific about topic, audience, and the kind of sources you trust. For example: "Open-access and .edu sources explaining how solar panels convert light to electricity, suitable for first-year college students."
    2. Choose Deep Research and start the search.
    3. Keep working while it runs. It can take several minutes. You can chat with your existing sources in the meantime.
    4. Read the report. It summarizes what was found, with citations to the sites it used.
    5. Import what you want. Add the sources that look useful to your notebook. You can also add the report itself as a source.
    6. Vet before you build. Open each imported source and delete or uncheck anything off-topic, outdated, or unreliable.
    Facilitator: Deep Research takes several minutes, so run it before the session and show your finished results rather than waiting live. Participants can start their own run and come back to it later.

    Workshop checkpoint: start a video now

    Video Overviews can take a long time to generate, sometimes more than 30 minutes. Once your sources are loaded, open the Studio, select Video Overview, and generate one with default settings. It builds in the background while we keep going. We'll review it in the video lab.

    Chat with your sources

    0:28 – 0:38

    The chat is where you ask questions and give instructions in plain language. It answers from the sources you have checked.

    Citations

    Answers contain small numbered markers. Hover over one to see the quoted passage; click it to jump to that spot in the source. Use citations to check accuracy, especially before you reuse an answer with students.

    Configure the chat

    Open the chat settings (Configure chat) to change how it responds.

    Default

    General research and brainstorming.

    Learning Guide

    Acts like a tutor: asks questions and breaks concepts into steps instead of just giving answers.

    Custom

    Write your own role and goal, for example "a lab instructor who quizzes me before explaining."

    You can also choose a shorter, default, or longer response length.

    Keep what's useful

    • Save to note keeps a response, with its citations, in the Studio panel.
    • Notes can be turned into sources, so your own summaries become part of the notebook.
    • Chat history is private to you and saved between sessions until you clear it.
    • Suggested chips under the chat box can start a Mind Map or other outputs directly.

    Try it

    Paste one of these into your notebook's chat.

    Find the main ideas
    What are the five most important ideas across these sources? For each, give a one-sentence explanation and cite where it appears.
    Spot disagreements
    Where do these sources disagree or emphasize different things? Quote the relevant passages.
    Find the hard parts
    Which concepts in these sources would a first-year student most likely misunderstand, and why?

    Every Studio item has a three-dot menu. Use View custom prompt there to see the instructions an item was made with.

    Mind Map

    Good for seeing how a topic fits together before you study the details. Mind maps are saved as notes in the Studio.

    Reports

    Choose Reports, then Document for a written report from a template or suggested format, or Interactive for a Learning Overview that can embed other Studio items. Select the pencil icon beside a template to customize it before generating. Interactive reports are web-only.

    Flashcards and Quiz

    Set difficulty to Easy, Medium, or Hard, and add a prompt to focus the questions. Both track your progress and let you review only what you missed. Retrieval practice like this usually does more for learning than passive listening, so pair it with your audio and video.

    Slide Deck

    Pick Detailed deck for a standalone handout or Presenter slides to talk over. To fix one slide, describe the change and only that slide is regenerated. Download as PDF or PowerPoint, or present full screen.

    Infographic

    Choose a level of detail (Concise, Standard, Detailed), an orientation (Square, Portrait, Landscape), and a style. Share by link or download a PNG. Always proofread the text in generated images.

    Audio Overviews

    0:45 – 1:05

    Podcast-style audio that discusses your sources, generated in a few minutes.

    Audio Overviews are designed to reflect what's in your sources rather than the hosts' opinions. You can listen in the browser, keep exploring your notebook while it plays, download it, or share a link.

    The four formats

    Deep Dive (default)

    Two hosts unpack and connect the ideas in a lively conversation. This is the classic format and the only one that currently supports Interactive mode.

    Use it for: introducing a unit, reviewing a chapter, making dense reading approachable.

    The Brief

    One speaker delivers the key takeaways in under two minutes.

    Use it for: a quick recap before class, a "what you missed" summary, an announcement-length overview.

    The Critique

    Two hosts give a constructive evaluation of the material, as an expert reviewer would.

    Use it for: feedback on your own draft syllabus, grant narrative, or assignment prompt. It works best when the source is something you wrote.

    The Debate

    Two hosts take opposing positions in a formal back-and-forth.

    Use it for: discussion starters, ethics cases, policy questions, or showing students how to argue from evidence.

    Build a custom audio prompt

    Interactive mode: join the conversation

    In Interactive mode you can interrupt the hosts with your voice and ask a question. They answer from your sources, then pick the conversation back up.

    1. Generate a new Deep Dive Audio Overview. Interactive mode only works on newly generated overviews.
    2. Select Interactive mode on the audio player.
    3. Press play, then select Join while it plays.
    4. When a host calls on you, ask your question out loud. Allow your browser to use the microphone.

    Know the limits

    • Interactive mode is English only.
    • Expect a short delay after you join or speak, and occasional voice glitches.
    • Your voice and the interaction aren't stored or shared. People you share the audio with hear the original version and can't join it themselves.

    Sharing audio

    • Download the audio file and post it in your LMS, OneDrive, Teams, or Classroom. This is the most reliable option, and students don't need a Google account.
    • Share a link from the player. The notebook must be shared with the listener, or public, with access to the full notebook.
    • Share the whole notebook so others find the audio in their Studio panel.

    Deleting an Audio Overview breaks any link you shared to it.

    Hands-on lab: make two audio versions

    About 15 minutes. You'll compare how format and prompt change the result.

    Facilitator: Ask two or three people to share what their prompt changed. Differences in audience and focus are usually the most noticeable. If rooms are noisy, have people use Interactive mode in pairs with one headset.

    Video Overviews

    1:05 – 1:20

    Narrated videos with generated visuals, built from the same sources.

    Where audio talks through your material, video shows it: diagrams, key quotes, and illustrations assembled into a narrated walkthrough. Generation happens in the background and can take a while, sometimes more than 30 minutes, so plan ahead.

    The three formats

    Explainer

    A structured, comprehensive overview that connects the ideas in your sources. Supports visual styles and many languages.

    Available on all plans

    Short

    A vertical video of roughly 60 seconds covering the key concepts. Made for phones.

    Ages 18+; rolled out to paid plans first

    Cinematic

    An immersive, animated, story-driven video. The AI decides the narrative and look.

    Ages 18+, English only; higher-tier paid plans

    Visual styles (Explainer)

    Choose a style to set the look. Select one to see where it tends to fit.

    Choose a style.

    Visual style choices apply to Explainer videos for users 18 and older. Cinematic and Short videos choose their own look.

    Watch, review, and share

    Open the video from the Studio panel. The player has playback speed, a scrub bar, skip buttons, full screen, and feedback buttons.

    Review before you publish

    Voices and visuals are AI-generated and can contain mistakes or glitches. Watch the whole video, check facts and on-screen text against your sources, and confirm captions or a transcript are available before posting it to a course. If the story is wrong, change your sources or prompt and regenerate. If only a small part is off, you can edit the downloaded file in a video editor.

    Share a video the same three ways as audio: download the file, share a link (the notebook must be shared or public), or share the whole notebook.

    Hands-on lab: review and steer a video

    About 15 minutes. Your first video should be ready from the checkpoint earlier.

    Facilitator: Second-round videos may not finish before the session ends. Tell participants their video will be waiting in the notebook later, and show a finished example you prepared in advance.

    Sharing, plans, and responsible use

    1:20 – 1:25

    Sharing a notebook

    • Privately: add people by email. They can explore your sources, chat, and see Studio items.
    • Publicly: "Anyone with a link" access. This works on personal Google accounts but is turned off for Google Workspace for Education and Enterprise accounts.
    • By file: download audio, video, slides, or infographics and post them wherever your students already are, such as your LMS, OneDrive, Teams, or Google Classroom. This is the best option when students don't have Google accounts.
    • You need edit access to a notebook to generate or delete Audio and Video Overviews.

    Gemini app connection

    Notebooks sync with the Notebooks section of the Gemini app, so you can build a notebook on the web and use it for quick chats on your phone.

    Responsible use

    Check accuracy

    Outputs can contain errors even with good sources. Verify claims with citations before sharing.

    Protect privacy

    Your account type sets your protections. On a personal account, never upload student records or personal information. On a school Google account, follow your institution's data rules.

    Respect copyright

    Upload only material you own, openly licensed content, or items you have permission to use.

    Be transparent

    Tell students when audio or video was AI-generated, and explain how you reviewed it.

    Make it accessible

    Provide a transcript for audio and captions for video before posting to a course.

    Mind age limits

    Cinematic and Short videos, and custom visual styles, are for users 18 and older.

    A useful habit

    Listening and watching feel productive but are passive. Pair each audio or video with a quiz, flashcards, or a discussion question so students actually use what they heard.

    Quick reference

    Take-away
    Glossary

    Notebook: a project containing its own sources, chat, notes, and Studio outputs.

    Source: a file, link, or text you add. The AI answers only from checked sources.

    Source-grounded: answers come from your sources and include citations.

    Studio: the panel that generates audio, video, and other materials.

    Audio Overview: AI-generated spoken discussion of your sources.

    Video Overview: AI-generated narrated video of your sources.

    Interactive mode: lets you ask the audio hosts questions by voice.

    Steering prompt: instructions that focus what a video covers.

    Fast / Deep Research: built-in tools that find web sources for you.

    Core Service: a Google Workspace for Education tool covered by the school's Google agreement. Gemini Notebook is one.

    Troubleshooting

    The audio or video ignored part of my material. Check which sources are selected, and name the topic in your prompt.

    A source didn't import well. Open it and read the summary. Scanned PDFs and videos without captions often fail; try a text version.

    There's no Join button. Interactive mode needs a newly generated Deep Dive in English.

    My video is taking forever. That's normal. Leave the page and come back; it keeps generating.

    I can't make a public link. Google Workspace for Education and work accounts block public sharing. Share with specific people, or download the file.

    My school Google account can't open Gemini Notebook. Your administrator may have turned it off. Contact your IT department.

    My school email won't sign in. Microsoft school accounts can't sign in to Google services. Use a personal Google account.

    I hit a daily limit. Limits reset daily. Choose carefully which items to regenerate.
